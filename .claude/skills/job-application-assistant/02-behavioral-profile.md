## Overview
Medet Ospanov's profile is based on self-assessment (no formal PI/DISC/MBTI test taken). The pattern is a **Builder-Delegator**: someone who spends years constructing systems and teams that function independently of their day-to-day presence, then steps back once the system is self-sustaining. Deliberate rather than impulsive, and energized by collaborative, friendly team environments rather than solo grind or rigid hierarchy.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Systems-building | High | Builds policies, processes, and teams designed to run without constant founder oversight (demonstrated across ACCREDO and Mediscan) |
| Team orientation | High | Thrives in a friendly, engaged, team-involved atmosphere; draws energy from collaborative work |
| Decisiveness pace | Deliberate | Prefers to weigh decisions rather than react quickly; comfortable taking time to reach the right call |
| Adaptability | High | Comfortable adapting to any team structure - close collaboration, solo work, or hands-off delegation - not wedded to one working mode |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Delegation through process, not through absence:** 9+ years building quality/patient-safety systems (policies, audits, corrective actions) designed as functioning management tools that keep running without the director hovering over them.
- **Founder-to-operator transition:** Built Mediscan from zero to a 4-clinic, ~50-staff network, then deliberately stepped back from day-to-day operations and the founding shareholder role once the system was self-sustaining (Jul 2026), to focus on ACCREDO full-time.
- **Self-directed technical upskilling:** Picked up AI agent development and workflow automation (Claude Code, n8n, Julius.ai) without a dedicated engineering team, and applies it directly to clinical/administrative operations.

## How You Work Best
- Friendly, team-involved atmosphere with genuine engagement, not a purely transactional or siloed setup
- Given ownership of a system or process end-to-end, with room to design the process rather than just execute someone else's
- Environments that value building durable, well-documented systems over constant firefighting

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Repetitive, unfixable processes drain energy:** Frame as a strength in disguise - a natural pull toward diagnosing and fixing broken processes rather than tolerating them, which is exactly the JCI/quality-improvement mindset.
- **Deliberate pace on decisions:** Frame as thoroughness and risk-awareness, valuable in regulated, patient-safety-critical environments where a fast wrong call has real cost.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

- "builds systems/processes", "quality culture", "collaborative team", "autonomy", "process ownership", "founder-led", "scale-up", "accreditation", "patient safety culture"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):

## Management Style Preferences
- Works best when given a mandate to build or fix a system and the autonomy to design how, checking in on outcomes rather than daily activity
- Does not work well long-term in roles that are pure repetitive execution with no path to improve the underlying process
